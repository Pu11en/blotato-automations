## Diagnostic map (verbatim)

A better way to improve is to open the audience retention graph and match the drops to the actual sentences in your script.

I usually think about it like this:

* If people leave immediately, the hook was probably unclear, too slow, or gave them no reason to care.
* If they stay for the hook but leave during the setup, you probably added too much background information before getting back to the conflict.
* If there is a drop right after the first big reveal, the reveal may have answered the main question without creating another one.
* If the graph slowly declines the entire time, the pacing may be too repetitive. The story is technically moving forward, but every section feels the same.
* If there is a spike or people replay one section, it could mean that moment was really interesting. It can also mean the sentence or visual was confusing, so people had to watch it again.
* If people leave right before the ending, they may have already predicted the payoff.
* If retention stays strong near the end, that usually means viewers still expect an important answer or consequence.

The useful part is matching this to the transcript instead of guessing.

For example, put timestamps next to every sentence:

0:00 - hook
0:04 - context
0:10 - first problem
0:18 - reveal
0:26 - reaction
0:35 - second complication
0:47 - ending

Then look at where the graph changes.

If there is a major drop at 0:10, don't immediately rewrite the entire story. Look at what happened around that sentence. Did you pause the conflict to explain someone's job? Did the captions become too long? Did the story answer the only interesting question?

I also think it helps to change one major thing at a time.

## Companion: 12-second opening template (u/Common-Post-8997, r/aitubers 1vp2zb4, 2026-08-15, 7 upvotes, no channel linked)

Second 0–3: Pattern interrupt (strong visual or surprising statement)
Second 3–7: Exact promise of what they will walk away with
Second 7–12: Why it matters right now + tiny proof or consequence of not knowing

"I stopped using AI for the first 30 seconds. I write those lines myself, then let AI help with the middle if needed. ... the hook is not the title. The hook is the first 12 seconds of spoken + visual. If those two don't match the thumbnail promise, people bounce."
